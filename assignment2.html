Spatial Control Prototypes

My first area of exploration is related to the spatial control of my synth. The spatial input allows users to move a marker around an area to change different characteristics of the sound. I wanted to explore different combinations of controls to see which ones could create interesting sounds while still being simple enough for toddlers to understand through experimentation.

I am concerned with a couple of different aspects of the spatial control. Firstly, approachability, as the controls should be easy to understand without requiring knowledge of technical music or synthesiser terminology. I am also interested in how the different controls can create noticeable changes to the sound and encourage the user to explore the space.

Prototype 1: Filter + Attack

This prototype uses the horizontal position to control the filter, making the sound more muffled on the left and brighter on the right. The vertical position controls the attack, with a short attack at the top and a long attack at the bottom. I wanted to start with these controls because they create noticeable changes to the sound while still giving the user a simple way to explore.

Prototype 2: Pitch + Echo

This prototype changes the controls to pitch and echo. Moving the marker from left to right changes the pitch from low to high, while moving it from top to bottom changes the amount of echo. This prototype is more suited for simpler child minds as it doesn't utilise complex parameter concepts, I wanted to explore these controls because pitch is easy to understand and echo can create more playful and noticeable changes to the sound.

Prototype 3: Four Sound Corners

This prototype is a more unique design, and gives each corner of the spatial area a different combination of sound characteristics. For example, one corner creates a soft sound with a low pitch and little echo, while another creates a buzzy sound with a high pitch and lots of echo. The sounds gradually change as the marker moves between the corners, creating different sounds to discover across the space.



Visual Feedback Prototypes

My second area of exploration is related to the visual feedback of the spatial input. While the spatial controls change the sound, I wanted to explore different ways the interface could show the user that their actions are having an effect. The visual feedback is important because it can help toddlers understand the connection between their actions and the sounds they create.

I am concerned with how the visual feedback can make the instrument feel more playful and understandable. I wanted to explore different levels of feedback, from changing the whole spatial area to only changing the marker. The three prototypes give me different ways to show the user that their position and actions are affecting the sound.

Prototype 1: Colour Gradient

This prototype changes the colour of the spatial input area depending on where the marker is positioned. Moving the marker around the area creates a gradual blend between different colours, while the marker remains visible. I wanted to explore colour as a simple form of feedback that could make the spatial input feel more playful.

Prototype 2: Shape Feedback

This prototype uses a large shape next to the spatial input area that changes depending on the marker's position. The shape becomes smaller or larger horizontally and changes between circular and angular shapes vertically. This provides a simple visual graphic for young minds to look at and base their experimentation off.

Prototype 3: Marker Feedback

Finally, this prototype keeps the spatial input area mostly the same and uses the marker itself to provide feedback. The marker changes size or colour depending on its position and pulses when a key is pressed. I wanted to explore this because it keeps the interface simple while still giving the user a clear visual response to their interactions.
